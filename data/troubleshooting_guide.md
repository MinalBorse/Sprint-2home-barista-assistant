# Coffee Troubleshooting Guide

This guide explains common taste problems in home-brewed coffee, why they happen, and how to fix them. Most taste problems come from extraction: how much flavour the water pulls out of the coffee grounds.

## Understanding extraction

Extraction is the process of water dissolving flavours from ground coffee. Sour and acidic flavours dissolve first, sweet flavours come next, and bitter flavours dissolve last.

- Under-extracted coffee has not had enough flavour pulled out. It tastes sour, sharp, thin or salty.
- Over-extracted coffee has had too much pulled out. It tastes bitter, harsh, burnt, dry or hollow.
- Well-extracted coffee tastes balanced and sweet.

Four things control extraction: grind size, water temperature, brew time and the coffee-to-water ratio.

## Sour, sharp, acidic or tangy coffee

Sour, sharp, acidic or tangy coffee is usually under-extracted. The water did not pull out enough of the sweet flavours.

How to fix it:
- Use a finer grind, so water extracts flavour faster.
- Use hotter water, between 92 and 96 degrees Celsius.
- Brew for longer.
- Use slightly less coffee for the same amount of water.

Note: light roasts are naturally brighter and more acidic. A pleasant, fruity acidity is normal. An unpleasant, sharp sourness is a sign of under-extraction.

## Bitter, harsh, burnt or astringent coffee

Bitter, harsh, burnt or astringent coffee is usually over-extracted. The water pulled out too many of the bitter compounds.

How to fix it:
- Use a coarser grind, so water extracts flavour more slowly.
- Use slightly cooler water, around 90 to 93 degrees Celsius.
- Brew for a shorter time.
- For a French press, steep for about 4 minutes, not longer, and pour all the coffee out right away so it stops brewing.

Note: dark roasts are naturally more bitter and smoky. If you dislike bitterness, try a medium or light roast.

## Weak, watery or thin coffee

Weak or watery coffee usually means too little coffee for the amount of water, or under-extraction.

How to fix it:
- Use more coffee. A common starting ratio is 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water (1:16).
- Use a finer grind.
- Make sure the coffee is fresh. Old coffee loses flavour.

## Strong, heavy or overpowering coffee

Coffee that tastes too strong usually has too much coffee for the amount of water.

How to fix it:
- Use less coffee or more water. Try moving from 1:15 to 1:17.
- Note that strong is not the same as bitter. Strong coffee can still taste balanced.

## Flat, dull or stale coffee

Flat or dull coffee is often caused by old or badly stored beans.

How to fix it:
- Use beans within 2 to 4 weeks of their roast date.
- Grind just before brewing. Ground coffee loses aroma within minutes.
- Store beans in an airtight container, away from light, heat and moisture.

## Quick reference

| Taste problem | Likely cause | First thing to try |
|---|---|---|
| Sour, sharp, acidic | Under-extraction | Grind finer |
| Bitter, harsh, burnt | Over-extraction | Grind coarser |
| Weak, watery | Too little coffee | Use more coffee |
| Too strong | Too much coffee | Use more water |
| Flat, dull | Stale beans | Use fresher beans |

Change only one thing at a time, so you can taste what each change does.
